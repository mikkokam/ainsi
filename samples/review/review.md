# A free gallery selling a ticket is a different building

For forty years the doors were open and nobody counted. Blue Hour put a price on one wing and kept the rest free, which turned every ordinary decision, where the shop sits, when the last entry is, how long a room takes to walk, into something a visitor could feel cheated by.

The season was the argument for doing it again. Not the takings on their own: whether the free rooms lost anything by having a paid one beside them.

> [!NOTE]
> Counts are from the door sensors, not the till. A visitor who bought a ticket and left after ten minutes is one visit, and is in the dwell figures too.

![A visitor alone in front of a lit print](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1531913764164-f85c52e6e654?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop)

# The season in four numbers

<!-- ainsi: figures -->
- **61 400** visits across the fourteen weeks, paid and free
- **73%** of paid visitors had never bought a ticket here before
- **41 min** median time in the paid wing, against 12 in the free rooms
- **9** days the last hour sold out before noon

# The queue nobody designed

Room three was built for nine prints and a bench. On the first Saturday it took a hundred and forty people an hour, and the queue for it ran back through room two, which is where the early work hangs and where nobody stopped again for fourteen weeks.

The fix is not a bigger room. It is timed entry to that room alone, in twenties, booked at the door on the day: the thing every visitor said they did not want and every visitor to a sold-out hour already had.

# What each failure cost

<!-- ainsi: boxes -->
1. **The roof, twice** €19 000 and four days of room five closed in the wettest fortnight
2. **The loan that never came** three prints short on the loan wall, and the catalogue already printed
3. **The queue** no cash figure, and the only thing anybody wrote to us about

# Where the money should go

<!-- ainsi: matrix x="what it costs" y="what it prevents" -->
- **Timed entry to room three** a tablet at the door and a trained steward
- **Roof, the north pitch** €55 000 in the yard, before September
- **A second front desk** cheap, and it prevents nothing anybody complained about
- **Extending the paid wing** the answer to a question nobody asked

# What happens before the doors open

<!-- ainsi: timeline -->
- **March** Loans confirmed in writing, catalogue held until they are.
- **May** North pitch of the roof done, while the rooms below are empty.
- **July** Timed entry rehearsed on the free rooms, where a mistake is cheap.
- **September** Stewards trained on the door, not the desk.
